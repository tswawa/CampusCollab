# CampusCollab Market and Demand Research

[简体中文](market-research.zh-CN.md) · [Master plan](../../PLAN.en.md) · [Competitive landscape](competitive-landscape.en.md) · [International adoption](international-adoption.en.md)

Research cutoff: 2026-10-08. The owner has accepted further development, publication on GitHub under AGPL, and both Chinese and English versions. The project currently has local development and testing only, with no institutional adoption. Other universities' usage is not evidence of this project's users or outcomes.

**Conclusion: this category has real institutional uses and warrants further investment. The strongest initial scope is practical activities and closeout work for departments and campus service teams: specify requirements, organize participation, collect evidence, resolve corrections, and produce results that can be reconciled and handed over.** Registration, image submission and approval tools are already common. The opportunity is to complete a specific job with less configuration and maintenance work.

## Scope and evidence levels

The primary demand sample contains ten institutions in nine Chinese provinces or municipalities: Liaoning, Hunan, Zhejiang, Shandong, Sichuan, Jiangsu, Beijing, Anhui and Guangdong. Repeated sources for the same institution and project are consolidated. Supplementary procurement, recognition and integration evidence is not added to that sample count. Separate reports examine eight representative competitors and six overseas universities. These are purposively selected cases for product decisions, not a random sample or a basis for national market, penetration or revenue estimates.

| Evidence type | What it supports | What it does not establish directly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student feedback and institutional responses | A problem was raised and a responsible unit publicly explained its causes | National incidence, a purely software cause, or certainty that this project will solve it |
| Completed-activity reports | An activity happened, with the institution's reported workflow and counts | Independent audit, software satisfaction, retention or causal time savings |
| System launch and training reports | A product entered an institutional process and intended users received training | Training attendance equals active users or sustained usage |
| Manuals and rules | Concrete fields, roles, recognition and handover requirements | Adoption scale or user experience |
| Procurement notices and award documents | The rationale or scope at the stated procurement stage | Notice equals award, award equals acceptance, or contract value equals this project's value |
| Source code and product documentation | Implementation on the inspected path or a vendor's stated capability | Successful deployment, adoption or comparative efficiency |

## Pain points and the useful scope of a solution

| Pain point | Evidence | Product work | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Missing evidence is discovered late and people must be contacted repeatedly | D09, D06 | Define evidence items before collection, show missing items per person and request specific corrections with version history | Completeness does not establish authenticity or entitlement to service credit |
| Rosters, evidence and final spreadsheets must be associated again | D04, D06, D10 | Import existing rosters, match stable identifiers and generate output from the final approved versions | Names and nicknames are not sufficient identifiers; real templates need mappings, not arbitrary CSV files |
| Participants need to know their result and correction progress | D09's delay complaints support better feedback; they do not directly establish that the existing platform lacks status views | Personal action lists, specific missing items and deadlines, notification links and visible processing states | A generated notification is not delivered email or a WeChat message |
| Group activities need both shared evidence and individual records | D06's organizer-level submission process establishes a workflow requirement, not widespread duplicate uploads | Coordinator confirmation, shared evidence linked once, separate participant outcomes | One group photo does not establish everyone's presence or identical duration |
| Responsibility becomes fragmented after personnel changes | Organizational-account and recognition rules below | Transfer open work, templates, records and operational responsibility, revoking former access | Another administrator account does not replace server, domain, backup and upgrade handover |

The first two points are the best initial experience: define final requirements early, target corrections and export without matching people again, finding final files or copying key fields. Notifications, handover and operations make that experience sustainable.

## Users and adopters

Direct users include coordinators, organizers and participants. Operators may be campus developers or an IT-owning unit. Formal recognition may belong to a college, student-affairs unit, volunteer office or course administrator. GitHub contributors may simply want to reuse the state machine, templates or deployment approach. These roles need not be held by the same person.

Open-source value includes inspectable workflows, editable templates, data control and reproducible delivery. AGPL does not create demand. Without a maintainer, self-hosting adds work. The product can prepare participation records, but an application role does not confer institutional recognition authority.

## Segment choices and alternative approaches

| Direction | Assessment | Choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distributed practical tasks and closeout for departments/campus services | Similar internally developed systems and concrete procedures exist; the current multi-participant core fits reasonably well | First choice, with an identified coordinator and operator |
| Volunteer evidence and roster preparation | Clear demand with strong registration-tool and incumbent-platform competition | A principal use case, focusing on final reconciliation and handover |
| One-off orientation registration | Forms and groups have very low setup costs | Not the main reason to adopt a self-hosted product |
| Repeated daily check-ins | Real use and mature substitutes; the current single-submission model is insufficient | Evaluate later against a specific need rather than expanding into a check-in platform |
| University-wide labor courses and academic credit | Multiple policy layers, institutional identity and migrations matter | Provide transferable results; a full replacement is a separate project |
| Formal teaching/laboratory assistant and work-study employment | Terms, eligibility, attendance, payroll and approvals differ structurally | Exclude payroll and employment management from 1.0 |
| Reusable open-source workflow implementation | States, permissions, templates and deployment can support developers even without direct institutional adoption | Pursue alongside the product, without calling it proven market success |

The conservative path publishes source and running instructions first, at lower cost, but leaves coordinators with configuration and preparation work. The recommended path completes a lightweight self-hosted product connecting templates, rosters, corrections, closeout and handover. The aggressive path builds a university-wide platform with much larger integration and long-term service obligations; it is not selected now.

## Connection to the implementation plan

The accepted scope includes all core improvements and English support. [PLAN.en.md](../../PLAN.en.md) is the execution reference: T03 template contracts; T04 identity/data migration; T05 through T08 internationalization and enrollment; T09 roster import; T10 through T17 evidence, review and outputs; T19 notifications; T20 handover; T21 retention; T22 through T27 operations and release; T28 referenced evidence reuse; T29 template contributions; T30 justified external adapters.

The first demonstrable outcome is a complete workflow: select deliverables, import or recruit participants, find missing items, request precise corrections, approve a specific version, and generate a roster, evidence and reconciliation checklist. Generic templates can start development. Institutional compatibility requires the original file, field definitions and an output-by-output comparison.

Use identical fictional data to compare duplicated entry, file searches, renaming, mismatches and active handling time in the old and new workflows. Record human authenticity review separately. Do not claim fixed percentage savings before measurement, and do not make finding an offline pilot a prerequisite for the product choices already supported by public evidence.

Revise the template or narrow the product if existing tools can be configured to produce the same output, operators still retype key fields, every template requires changes to core code, or new accounts and uploads offset the benefit. If the only remaining benefit is renaming and compression, a local preparation tool may fit better.

## Conclusion and maintenance

Public evidence supports a bounded bilingual product that connects actual organizational tasks, participation, evidence and final records. A distinctive advantage still needs implementation and reproducible demonstration; GitHub name counts, training attendance and procurement totals cannot substitute for it.

Updates should retain source dates, evidence types, affected product decisions and validation status. Update mappings when institutional rules or templates change. Another citation does not automatically add an independent market case. This request saves research and plans only; application implementation, licensing changes, deployment and GitHub publication have not been performed.
